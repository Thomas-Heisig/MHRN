# Semantic Prototype Consolidation Does Not Outperform Matched Raw Replay in a Preregistered Split-MNIST Regime

## Abstract

Continual-learning systems must preserve prior task performance while acquiring new information. A previous preregistered MHRN mechanism experiment, EXP-S6-SEM-CL-001, found that semantic prototype consolidation with bounded replay improved Split-MNIST retention relative to a naive online learner without replay. The present confirmatory experiment asked a stricter question: whether semantic prototypes themselves provide an advantage over equally budgeted raw-experience replay. We preregistered four conditions—naive online learning, raw replay, semantic-prototype replay, and matched random-prototype replay—using nine paired seeds, fixed object/update budgets, deterministic spike coding, an identical online readout, and a conjunctive success rule. The semantic condition failed all three preregistered minimum-effect criteria. Relative to raw replay, the final-accuracy difference was -0.000056 (95% paired bootstrap CI -0.002224 to +0.001722; exact paired sign-flip p=1.0) and the forgetting advantage was -0.000278 (CI -0.002986 to +0.001944; p=1.0). Relative to random prototypes, semantic replay produced a statistically positive but very small +0.001778 accuracy difference (CI +0.000833 to +0.002944; p=0.015625), far below the preregistered +0.03 minimum. We therefore falsify the directed semantic-prototype advantage hypothesis for this protocol. The result limits interpretation of the earlier positive experiment but does not prove that the earlier effect was exclusively a generic replay effect because the two protocols use different replay exposures. The study demonstrates the value of preregistered negative results for separating representation quality from replay exposure in MHRN development.

## 1. Research question

**RQ-S6-SEM-002:** Does MHRN semantic prototype consolidation reduce catastrophic forgetting more than equally memory- and update-budgeted raw-experience replay?

The directed hypothesis H1 predicted a practically meaningful semantic advantage. H0 predicted no difference. A counter-hypothesis allowed raw replay to match or outperform semantic prototypes because raw experience may preserve information lost during compression.

## 2. Design

The experiment used five Split-MNIST tasks: 0/1, 2/3, 4/5, 6/7 and 8/9. Seeds 201–209 were paired across all four conditions. Input coding, task indices and readout architecture were shared.

The replay conditions were:

- **B2 Raw replay:** stored observed spike-coded training patterns;
- **B3 Semantic prototype:** stored mature prototypes generated by the existing MHRN `SemanticMemory` mechanism;
- **B4 Random prototype:** stored random spike patterns matched object-by-object to B3 in task, label, feature dimension and spike count.

B1 was a naive no-replay reference.

A pre-execution amendment resolved ambiguities before runner implementation. The nominal capacity N=50 was defined as a per-task ceiling. The realized replay-object count `R_t` was set by the number of mature B3 concepts and mirrored exactly in B2 and B4. Replay conditions received exactly K=100 replay updates at each of four task transitions, for a maximum of 400 replay updates per seed.

## 3. Pre-execution controls

Before empirical execution:

1. the original preregistration was merged;
2. amendment A1 was merged;
3. runner and analysis code were implemented;
4. 19 synthetic tests passed;
5. execution without authorization was verified to fail;
6. seven source files were SHA-256 frozen;
7. the freeze was attested;
8. a separate authorization permitted exactly one empirical run.

The empirical workflow verified all seven frozen hashes before loading MNIST.

## 4. Preregistered success rule

A positive semantic-specific result required all of the following:

1. B3 − B2 final average accuracy ≥ +0.03;
2. B2 − B3 mean forgetting ≥ +0.05;
3. B3 − B4 final average accuracy ≥ +0.03;
4. lower bound of each paired 95% bootstrap interval > 0;
5. paired two-sided exact sign-flip p < 0.05 for all three contrasts;
6. at least nine complete paired seeds.

No confirmatory endpoint, threshold or exclusion criterion was modified after data access.

## 5. Results

All nine seeds completed in all four conditions.

### 5.1 Semantic vs raw replay: final accuracy

B3 − B2 final average accuracy was **-0.000056**. The 95% paired bootstrap interval was **[-0.002224, +0.001722]** and the exact paired sign-flip test gave **p=1.0**. The result fails the +0.03 minimum effect, the positive-interval criterion and the significance criterion.

### 5.2 Semantic vs raw replay: forgetting

B2 − B3 mean forgetting was **-0.000278**, meaning the point estimate is very slightly unfavorable to semantic replay. The 95% interval was **[-0.002986, +0.001944]**, with **p=1.0**. The result fails the +0.05 minimum effect, interval and significance criteria.

### 5.3 Semantic vs random prototypes

B3 − B4 final average accuracy was **+0.001778** with a 95% interval of **[+0.000833, +0.002944]** and **p=0.015625**. Although this difference is statistically detectable under the frozen analysis, it is only about 0.18 percentage points and therefore fails the preregistered +3 percentage-point minimum effect by a wide margin.

### 5.4 Confirmatory decision

The conjunctive primary rule fails. The directed H1 is therefore **falsified under this protocol**. The result is retained as negative evidence for the proposed semantic-prototype advantage in the tested regime.

## 6. Interpretation

The central result is not merely “non-significant”. The study was designed around minimum effects of practical interest. Semantic replay failed to show the required advantage over raw replay in either final accuracy or forgetting. The random-control contrast suggests that semantic structure can carry a small signal relative to random patterns, but this signal is far below the magnitude required to support the mechanism claim.

The result therefore separates two propositions that should not be conflated:

- semantic replay may differ measurably from random replay;
- semantic compression provides a useful continual-learning advantage over raw replay.

Only the first receives a small signal in CL-002; the second is not supported.

## 7. Relation to the positive CL-001 result

CL-001 showed a substantial benefit of semantic consolidation plus bounded replay over a naive online baseline. CL-002 does not invalidate that observation. It changes what can responsibly be inferred from it.

Because CL-002 does not show superiority over raw replay, the CL-001 improvement cannot currently be claimed as evidence that semantic compression itself caused the benefit. At the same time, the two experiments differ in replay exposure and update accounting, so CL-002 cannot establish that CL-001 was solely a generic replay effect. A new experiment is required to isolate replay dose while retaining matched raw/semantic controls.

## 8. Limitations

The experiment does not test a complete recurrent MHRN SNN against an established standard-SNN continual-learning system. It uses Split-MNIST only. The current semantic mechanism realizes only a few mature concepts per task under this configuration, well below the nominal object-capacity ceiling. Replay exposure is fixed at 100 updates per task transition. Results therefore apply to this bounded mechanism regime and should not be generalized to other replay doses, memory metrics, datasets or semantic-construction rules.

## 9. Scientific value of the negative result

The negative result removes an unsupported stronger claim and identifies the next causal variable that must be isolated: replay exposure. This is progress rather than a failed demonstration. The experiment also validates the repository's preregistration workflow: a positive predecessor was followed by a stricter comparison capable of falsifying the preferred interpretation, and the resulting falsification was retained and published.

## 10. Conclusion

EXP-S6-SEM-CL-002 provides no confirmatory evidence that the current MHRN semantic-prototype consolidation outperforms matched raw-experience replay on Split-MNIST under the frozen memory and replay budget. H1 is falsified for this protocol. Future work should test replay-dose dependence and then move to a genuine standard-SNN baseline and an additional dataset, without modifying or rerunning CL-002 as a confirmatory study.
